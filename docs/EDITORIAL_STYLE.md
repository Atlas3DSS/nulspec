# Public editorial style

NULSPEC publishes independent replications for researchers, engineers, funders,
and industry teams. Public copy should help readers understand what was tested,
what happened, what remains uncertain, and what evidence is available. The aim
is to make replication easier to evaluate and reuse so the field can learn
faster.

## Scope statements

NULSPEC currently replicates computational machine-learning studies because
the team operates the compute and expertise required for that work. Public copy
must not imply a current cross-domain program or claim expertise, equipment, or
capacity that the team does not yet have. Any later expansion requires a
versioned policy change after the ML replication corpus and its selection and
escalation methods are mature.

## Required style

- Lead with the subject, measurement, outcome, or action. Do not withhold the
  point to create curiosity.
- Use the established scientific term for a result. Say `failed`, `null`,
  `contradictory`, `inconclusive`, `not measured`, or `not yet published` when
  that is the actual state.
- Distinguish observations, inferences, limitations, and future work.
- State what a chart encodes, which values share a scale, and what uncertainty
  an interval does and does not estimate.
- Use headings as factual labels or concise claims. Questions are appropriate
  only when the interface requires an answer, such as a form prompt.
- Prefer complete sentences, active voice, concrete nouns, and specific verbs.
- Explain practical significance when it helps the reader understand how a
  result should affect further research or industry decisions.
- Call model-based packet checks `automated consistency audits` or `release
  QA`. Do not present agreement between models as peer review, scientific
  validation, independent domain expertise, or additional replication.
- Keep a professional human voice. Technical precision does not require dense
  language or an impersonal tone.

## Prohibited style

- Do not use deliberate alliteration, wordplay, or stacked slogans. The approved
  thesis below is the only permitted rhyming line.
- Do not use euphemisms for experimental states, operational constraints,
  money, personal data, or unavailable evidence.
- Do not use slang, memes, cultural references, or language associated with
  promotional posts and engagement-driven headlines.
- Do not frame a scientific result as a reveal, win, surprise, vibe, or story.
  Prefer a specific measured state over shorthand such as `miss`.
- Do not use metaphors such as putting a claim "on the bench," keeping an
  apparatus "alive," buying "runway," or placing extensions behind a "fence."
- Do not use a contrast merely for rhetorical force. A contrast is appropriate
  when it communicates a real scientific distinction, such as an arm-level
  label versus a study-level classification.

The approved home-page thesis is **Published claims, independently tested.**
Use it once on the home page. Do not create alternate slogan-like versions.

## Publication data

Calibrated verdicts, findings, and limitations in a publication bundle are part
of the scientific record. Frontend work must render them accurately rather than
rewrite them for tone. If their wording needs correction, update them through
the study review and publication workflow so hashes and provenance remain
valid.

## Examples

| Avoid | Use |
| --- | --- |
| See the spread before reading the decimals. | Reward-difference estimates and conditional 95% intervals. |
| Publish the miss. | Report every outcome. |
| Put a claim on the bench. | Nominate an arXiv paper for replication. |
| The vote relay timed out. | The vote submission timed out. |
| Deeper evidence is not yet public. | Detailed attempt records are not yet public. |
| Support buys compute, not conclusions. | Funding increases replication capacity and does not affect verdicts. |

Review all visible copy, metadata, accessibility labels, form states, and error
messages against this standard before publication.
